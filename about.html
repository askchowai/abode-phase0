About Abode

Every house has a page, a feed, and a memory.

The idea

Listing sites treat a house as a product that exists for the ninety days it is for sale. Then the listing expires and everything anybody knew about the place disappears with it — the roof that was replaced in 2016, the corner of the lot that floods every seventh spring, the renovation the last owner documented in thirty-one posts.

Abode gives every address a permanent page instead. Search works the way you expect. Underneath it, the house keeps its record: sales, permits, storms, price cuts, and what the owner, the last owner, the tenant and the neighbours actually said. Follow an address the way you would follow a person.

Why the badges matter

A verified-owner badge is worth something only if it is hard to get. We match a claimant against the recorded deed and confirm with a mailed code or a document a person reads. Agents verify a state licence. Everyone else posts as a neighbour, and says so.

What this build is

This is a working front end running on sample data — eighteen addresses, their histories, their walls and their offers. Search, the map, verification, offers, groups, messages, alerts and moderation all work; they just run against records in the browser rather than an MLS feed and a database. Nothing here is a real listing and no offer made here is a contract.

What it would take to be real

Three things run on other people’s timelines: MLS licensing through a RESO aggregator, a canonical parcel identifier so an address keeps one page across decades and owners, and an owner-verification pipeline with mail and document review. The rest is ordinary engineering.
