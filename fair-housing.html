Fair housing

Abode is committed to the letter and the spirit of the Fair Housing Act. Housing discrimination is illegal, and a site where neighbours write about houses is a place where it will be attempted. This page says what we do about that.

What the law protects

Federal law prohibits discrimination in housing on the basis of race, colour, religion, sex (including sexual orientation and gender identity), national origin, familial status and disability. Many states and cities protect more than that — source of income, age, marital status, military status. Where a state protects more, the stricter rule applies here.

What that means for what you write

The rule is simple: describe the property, not the people you think belong in it. A post can say a house has four bedrooms, two flights of stairs and no fenced yard. A post cannot say who a house is or is not for.

  • No preferences about who should live somewhere — including "adults only", "no children", "perfect for a young family", or naming a religion or ethnicity as a feature of a block.
  • No steering. Describing one neighbourhood as "safe" or "good" and another as "rough" is read as a proxy for race in fair-housing enforcement, whatever you meant by it. Cite the source — the crime statistics, the test scores, the district map — and let people read it themselves.
  • No refusing to deal. An owner or agent who declines to answer a buyer because of a protected characteristic loses their badge and is reported.

What we check

Every post, reply and direct message on this site passes a screen before it is stored. Language that states a protected-class preference is blocked outright, with the rule quoted and a lawful way to say the same thing. Coded language gets a warning you can override, because context matters and a machine cannot always tell. Every block and every override is logged.

A screen is a speed bump, not a compliance programme. Reports go to a person.

If you were discriminated against

Report the post here so we act on the account, and file with HUD at hud.gov/fairhousing or 1-800-669-9777. Our report record is available to you and to an investigator on request.
